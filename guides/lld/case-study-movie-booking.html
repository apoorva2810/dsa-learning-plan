Case study: movie ticket booking

Seat booking is the LLD question where the class model meets a real race: hundreds of people want the same seats, and they pay real money. This chapter scopes the problem, models Movie, Theatre, Screen, Show, Seat, SeatLock, Booking and Payment, and then works through the hard part: when to claim a seat (a hold with a TTL or an optimistic check at payment), how holds expire (lazy checks or a sweeper), late payments, and idempotent confirmation. It ends with a tested Python implementation, the database guards that replace in-memory locks across servers, common extensions and a timed practice round.

After this chapter you can
  • Scope the problem with functional and non-functional requirements and state the invariant: one confirmed booking per (show, seat)
  • Model the entities so that availability lives per show and seat, not on the physical seat
  • Compare a hold at selection with an optimistic version check at payment, and size the hold TTL with Little's law
  • Explain lazy expiry versus a background sweeper, and handle a payment that succeeds after its hold ended
  • Make payment confirmation idempotent with idempotency keys, conditional transitions and webhook event dedupe
  • Implement and test a thread-safe booking core in Python, and name the database guard for each step of the flow

Requirements and scope

Design a movie ticket booking system (BookMyShow, Fandango, a cinema chain's own app) is a favourite LLD question because it has a small model you can code in an hour and a real concurrency problem: at on-sale time hundreds of people want the same few seats, and they pay real money. A strong answer spends about five minutes on requirements, ten on the model, and most of the rest on one question: what happens when two people pick the same seat?

Ask the interviewer, then write the scope down. The two checklists are a sensible scope for a 45 to 90 minute round; tick items off as you agree them.

The modelling decision that matters is that a Seat is physical and shared by every show in its screen. Is A7 taken? only has an answer for a (show, seat) pair. Put a flag on Seat and booking the 18:00 show also books the 21:00 one. The per-show state needs its own home: a SeatLock for a hold, a booked marker for a sale, or in a database one show_seats row per seat per show.

Seat selection under concurrency

▶ Video lesson: Alice and Bob pick seat A7 3:59 · 6 scenes · narrated, pause anytime and explore

The race is the check-then-act from Concurrency & thread safety, which covers locks, compare-and-swap and the database guards in general. What is specific to booking is the size of the window. From opening the seat map to finishing payment takes minutes, so on a popular show two people overlapping is the normal case, not bad luck. The design question is when in the flow you claim the seat, and what the loser goes through.

The simulation runs three answers on the same two people:

  • No claim until payment. Check at selection, write at confirmation. Wrong, and worth seeing why.
  • Hold at selection. Selecting a seat creates a SeatLock with a TTL (time to live: the hold ends on its own after that long). The claim comes first and the slow work after, so this is a pessimistic reservation.
  • Optimistic at payment. Nothing is held. The seat row carries a version, and paying claims the seat only if the version is unchanged since the user saw it. The claim comes last.

A hold costs availability (seats sit in abandoned carts until the TTL) and buys a good experience for the loser. Almost every ticketing site makes that trade.

'Why hold seats at selection instead of checking at payment?' A strong answer: 'Both are correct if the claim is atomic; the difference is when the loser finds out. With a hold, the second person is told on the click and picks another seat. With an optimistic check they find out after typing card details, and at an on-sale most people would. So I hold at selection with a TTL, show a countdown, and accept that abandoned holds block seats for a few minutes. The TTL is the knob that trades availability against time to pay.'

How long should a hold last?

The TTL trades two failures against each other. Too short, and people still on a slow payment page (3-D Secure, an OTP, a UPI approval on another phone) lose their seats while paying, which produces late payments that must be refunded. Too long, and abandoned carts make a show look sold out while seats sit unpaid. Little's law (the average number of items in a system equals their arrival rate times the time each one stays) sizes the second problem: seats tied up by holds = seats put on hold per minute × the average minutes a hold lives.

Checkout timers on ticketing sites are usually several minutes, in the 5 to 15 range as an order of magnitude. Pick one longer than the slow tail of your payment flow, show it as a countdown, and cap the seats one user can hold per show so that a bot cannot keep a show off sale.

Seats tied up by holds (Little's law)

Paid holds live for the payment time; abandoned ones live for the whole TTL. Raise the TTL and watch the seats that look sold out but are sitting in abandoned carts.

The booking flow: hold, pay, confirm

▶ Video lesson: Hold, pay, confirm 3:36 · 6 scenes · narrated, pause anytime and explore

The flow is three operations on the BookingService. Notice where the atomic steps are: two short ones, with a slow external call between them that holds no lock.

  1. hold_seats(user, show, seats): one atomic check-and-write on the show's seat state. It creates a PENDING Booking whose expires_at is the hold's deadline.
  2. pay(booking, idempotency_key): check that the hold is still live, charge the card through the gateway (seconds to minutes), then commit, a second atomic step that turns the holds into booked seats.
  3. Expiry: an unpaid hold just ends at its deadline. A sweeper later marks the booking EXPIRED.

Two short atomic steps (hold, commit) around one slow call (charge). Nothing is locked while the bank works.

Never hold a lock across the payment

A payment takes seconds to minutes and sometimes never returns. A mutex, a SELECT ... FOR UPDATE row lock or an open transaction held across it blocks every other request for those seats, ties up a database connection per waiting user, and turns one slow bank into a stalled show. The hold is the lock that lasts minutes, and it is only data with a deadline. Real locks last milliseconds: one for the hold, one for the commit.

Every arrow is a conditional update (WHERE status = 'PENDING' and so on), so two racing events can never both move the same booking.

When holds expire: sweeper or lazy expiry

▶ Video lesson: When a hold runs out 4:20 · 6 scenes · narrated, pause anytime and explore

A hold has to end even when nobody calls release: the user closes the tab, the phone loses signal, the app server crashes mid-request. There are two ways to make ended true.

  • Lazy expiry: store expires_at and make every check treat a hold whose deadline has passed as absent. Nothing has to run; the hold is over the instant the clock passes the deadline. The cost is discipline, because every query that asks is this seat free? must include the time, and there is no trigger for side effects, because nothing happens at expiry.
  • Background sweeper: a job runs every N seconds, finds ended holds, deletes them, marks their bookings EXPIRED and fires the side effects (the email, the waitlist). Queries stay simple, but a seat stays blocked for up to N seconds after its hold ended, and if the job stops, seats stay held.

Both halves in SQL (PostgreSQL)

# Lazy expiry: the claim itself treats an ended hold as free. All seats or none: compare the row count
# with the number of seats asked for, and roll back if it is short.
HOLD_SEATS = """
UPDATE show_seats
   SET status = 'HELD', booking_id = %(booking_id)s,
       hold_expires_at = now() + interval '10 minutes', version = version + 1
 WHERE show_id = %(show_id)s
   AND seat_id = ANY(%(seat_ids)s)
   AND (status = 'AVAILABLE' OR (status = 'HELD' AND hold_expires_at <= now()))
RETURNING seat_id
"""

# The seat map applies the same rule, so an ended hold shows as available at once.
SEAT_MAP = """
SELECT seat_id,
       CASE WHEN status = 'HELD' AND hold_expires_at <= now() THEN 'AVAILABLE' ELSE status END AS shown
  FROM show_seats
 WHERE show_id = %(show_id)s
"""

# The sweeper: clean-up and side effects, in small batches. SKIP LOCKED passes over rows that a hold or
# commit is changing right now, and the predicate cannot match a seat that has just been booked.
SWEEP_ENDED_HOLDS = """
WITH ended AS (
    SELECT show_id, seat_id, booking_id
      FROM show_seats
     WHERE status = 'HELD' AND hold_expires_at <= now()
     ORDER BY hold_expires_at
     LIMIT 500
       FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED
)
UPDATE show_seats s
   SET status = 'AVAILABLE', booking_id = NULL, hold_expires_at = NULL, version = s.version + 1
  FROM ended e
 WHERE s.show_id = e.show_id AND s.seat_id = e.seat_id
RETURNING e.booking_id
"""

One predicate, hold_expires_at <= now(), shared by the claim, the map and the sweeper.

The race at the deadline: a late payment

Expiry creates one case that every booking system must handle. The customer starts paying with two minutes left, the bank's page takes four, and the payment succeeds after the hold ended. By then the seat may still be free, or someone else may hold it. The rule: if the seat is still this booking's or free, confirm, because the customer has paid; if anyone else has it, refund automatically and say so. Never take the seat back from its new holder.

Idempotent payment confirmation

Around payments, duplicate signals are routine:

  • the user double-clicks Pay, or the app times out and retries the request;
  • the gateway retries its webhook until it gets a 2xx answer, so the same event can arrive twice;
  • the browser redirect (payment done, back to the shop) and the webhook report the same success, often within a second of each other;
  • your own worker crashes after the charge and before saving, then runs again.

Each case must end in one charge, one confirmation and one ticket email. Four layers do it, each catching what the one before cannot:

  1. An idempotency key per payment attempt, created by the client when the payment page opens and sent with every retry. The server stores key → result and replays the result for a repeat.
  2. The same key on the gateway call. Payment providers dedupe on it (Stripe's Idempotency-Key header, for example), so a charge retried after a lost response is not charged twice.
  3. A conditional state transition. Confirming is UPDATE bookings SET status = 'CONFIRMED' WHERE booking_id = ? AND status = 'PENDING'. One row changed: you confirmed it. Zero rows: the other signal got there first, so do nothing. Only the request that made the transition sends tickets.
  4. Webhook event ids recorded under a unique key, in the same transaction as the state change, so a redelivered event is recognised and acknowledged without doing anything.
flowchart TD
  E[Payment succeeded<br/>webhook or redirect] --> D{Event id<br/>seen before?}
  D -->|yes| ACK[Answer 200<br/>do nothing]
  D -->|no| S{Booking<br/>status?}
  S -->|CONFIRMED or CANCELLED| ACK
  S -->|PENDING or EXPIRED| C{Every seat still ours<br/>or free?}
  C -->|yes| OK[Book seats and confirm<br/>queue the tickets]
  C -->|no| RF[Mark EXPIRED<br/>queue a refund]
  OK --> ACK
  RF --> ACK
One handler for every success signal. Whatever order the duplicates arrive in, exactly one of them books the seats or queues the refund.

The handler behind the flowchart (psycopg 3, PostgreSQL)

CLAIM_EVENT = "INSERT INTO payment_events (event_id) VALUES (%s) ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING"
LOCK_BOOKING = "SELECT show_id, seat_ids, status FROM bookings WHERE booking_id = %s FOR UPDATE"
SET_STATUS = "UPDATE bookings SET status = %s WHERE booking_id = %s"
OUTBOX = "INSERT INTO outbox (kind, booking_id) VALUES (%s, %s)"
COMMIT_SEATS = """
UPDATE show_seats
   SET status = 'BOOKED', booking_id = %(booking_id)s, hold_expires_at = NULL, version = version + 1
 WHERE show_id = %(show_id)s AND seat_id = ANY(%(seat_ids)s)
   AND (   (status = 'HELD' AND booking_id = %(booking_id)s)       -- still ours, even a little late
        OR  status = 'AVAILABLE'                                    -- swept, and nobody took it
        OR (status = 'HELD' AND hold_expires_at <= now()))          -- someone else's hold has ended too
RETURNING seat_id
"""


class SeatsGone(Exception):
    pass


def on_payment_succeeded(conn, event_id: str, booking_id: int) -> str:
    """Called by the webhook and by the browser redirect. Safe to run any number of times."""
    with conn.transaction():
        if conn.execute(CLAIM_EVENT, (event_id,)).rowcount == 0:
            return "duplicate event: already handled"
        show_id, seat_ids, status = conn.execute(LOCK_BOOKING, (booking_id,)).fetchone()
        if status not in ("PENDING", "EXPIRED"):
            return f"booking already {status}"                     # the other signal got here first
        try:
            with conn.transaction():                                # a savepoint: every seat or none
                got = conn.execute(COMMIT_SEATS, dict(show_id=show_id, seat_ids=seat_ids,
                                                      booking_id=booking_id)).fetchall()
                if len(got) != len(seat_ids):
                    raise SeatsGone
        except SeatsGone:
            conn.execute(SET_STATUS, ("EXPIRED", booking_id))
            conn.execute(OUTBOX, ("refund", booking_id))           # a worker refunds with key f"refund-{booking_id}"
            return "seats gone: refund queued"
        conn.execute(SET_STATUS, ("CONFIRMED", booking_id))
        conn.execute(OUTBOX, ("send_tickets", booking_id))         # sent once per confirmation, even after a crash
        return "confirmed"
The booking row lock serialises the webhook and the redirect; the savepoint makes a multi-seat commit all or nothing; the outbox makes side effects happen once per state change.

Three keys, three jobs

Each duplicate arrives through a different door, so each needs its own dedupe. The client's idempotency key dedupes requests (a retried POST /pay). The webhook event id dedupes notifications (the same event delivered twice). The booking's status column dedupes outcomes (two different signals about one payment). Drop any one and a duplicate gets through.

The Python implementation

The whole design in four files: Python 3.10+, standard library only, matching the class diagram. SeatLockService is the in-memory stand-in for the show_seats table: one mutex per show plays the part of the database's row locks, and the clock is injected so tests can jump forward instead of sleeping. Expiry is lazy, and the late-payment rule is in commit.

Every method takes the show's mutex, reads the clock inside it, and decides with one helper, _can_take, so hold and commit can never disagree about what free means.

class SeatUnavailable(Exception):
    def __init__(self, show_id: str, seat_ids: list[str]):
        super().__init__(f"show {show_id}: not available: {', '.join(seat_ids)}")
        self.seat_ids = seat_ids


@dataclass
class _ShowSeats:
    """All seat state of one show, guarded by one mutex."""
    mutex: threading.Lock = field(default_factory=threading.Lock)
    holds: dict[str, SeatLock] = field(default_factory=dict)     # seat_id -> latest hold, possibly ended
    booked: dict[str, str] = field(default_factory=dict)         # seat_id -> booking_id


class SeatLockService:
    """Decides who has a seat for a show. One mutex per show makes a multi-seat check-and-write
    atomic and all-or-nothing, with no lock ordering to get wrong. Expiry is lazy: an ended hold
    counts as absent in every check, so correctness never waits for sweep_expired()."""

    def __init__(self, ttl: timedelta = timedelta(minutes=10),
                 clock: Callable[[], datetime] = datetime.now):
        self._ttl = ttl
        self._clock = clock
        self._registry = threading.Lock()                        # guards _shows
        self._shows: dict[str, _ShowSeats] = {}

    def _state(self, show_id: str) -> _ShowSeats:
        with self._registry:
            state = self._shows.get(show_id)
            if state is None:
                state = self._shows[show_id] = _ShowSeats()
            return state

    @staticmethod
    def _can_take(state: _ShowSeats, seat_id: str, booking_id: str, now: datetime) -> bool:
        owner = state.booked.get(seat_id)
        if owner is not None:
            return owner == booking_id                           # booked: only a repeat commit by the owner
        hold = state.holds.get(seat_id)
        return hold is None or hold.booking_id == booking_id or hold.is_expired(now)

    def hold(self, show_id: str, seat_ids: Iterable[str], user_id: str, booking_id: str) -> list[SeatLock]:
        seats = sorted(set(seat_ids))
        state = self._state(show_id)
        with state.mutex:
            now = self._clock()
            taken = [s for s in seats if not self._can_take(state, s, booking_id, now)]
            if taken:
                raise SeatUnavailable(show_id, taken)            # all or nothing: nothing was written
            locks = [SeatLock(show_id, s, user_id, booking_id, now + self._ttl) for s in seats]
            for lock in locks:
                state.holds[lock.seat_id] = lock                 # replaces an ended hold, if any
            return locks

    def is_held(self, show_id: str, seat_ids: Iterable[str], booking_id: str) -> bool:
        state = self._state(show_id)
        with state.mutex:
            now = self._clock()
            for s in seat_ids:
                hold = state.holds.get(s)
                if hold is None or hold.booking_id != booking_id or hold.is_expired(now):
                    return False
            return True

    def commit(self, show_id: str, seat_ids: Iterable[str], booking_id: str) -> bool:
        """Turn holds into bookings. Succeeds if every seat is still ours (even a little late)
        or free again; writes nothing if another booking has any of them."""
        seats = sorted(set(seat_ids))
        state = self._state(show_id)
        with state.mutex:
            now = self._clock()
            if not all(self._can_take(state, s, booking_id, now) for s in seats):
                return False
            for s in seats:
                state.holds.pop(s, None)
                state.booked[s] = booking_id
            return True

    def release(self, show_id: str, seat_ids: Iterable[str], booking_id: str) -> None:
        """Payment failed or the user gave up. Idempotent, and never touches another booking's hold."""
        state = self._state(show_id)
        with state.mutex:
            for s in seat_ids:
                hold = state.holds.get(s)
                if hold is not None and hold.booking_id == booking_id:
                    del state.holds[s]

    def unbook(self, show_id: str, seat_ids: Iterable[str], booking_id: str) -> None:
        """A cancellation puts this booking's seats back on sale."""
        state = self._state(show_id)
        with state.mutex:
            for s in seat_ids:
                if state.booked.get(s) == booking_id:
                    del state.booked[s]

    def seat_map(self, show_id: str, seat_ids: Iterable[str]) -> dict[str, str]:
        state = self._state(show_id)
        with state.mutex:
            now = self._clock()
            view = {}
            for s in seat_ids:
                hold = state.holds.get(s)
                if s in state.booked:
                    view[s] = "booked"
                elif hold is not None and not hold.is_expired(now):
                    view[s] = "held"
                else:
                    view[s] = "available"                        # an ended hold reads as free
            return view

    def sweep_expired(self) -> list[SeatLock]:
        """Housekeeping and side effects (emails, waitlist). Correctness never depends on it."""
        with self._registry:
            states = list(self._shows.values())
        ended: list[SeatLock] = []
        for state in states:
            with state.mutex:
                now = self._clock()
                for seat_id, hold in list(state.holds.items()):
                    if hold.is_expired(now):
                        del state.holds[seat_id]
                        ended.append(hold)
        return ended

pay claims the booking in a short critical section, charges with no lock held, then commits. Status changes go through _transition, the in-memory version of a conditional UPDATE.

import threading
from typing import Protocol
from uuid import uuid4

from models import Booking, BookingStatus, Payment, PaymentStatus, Show
from seat_locks import SeatLockService

MAX_SEATS = 10


class PaymentGateway(Protocol):
    def charge(self, amount: int, idempotency_key: str) -> Payment: ...   # charges at most once per key
    def refund(self, payment_id: str) -> None: ...


class PaymentInProgress(Exception):
    """Another request is already paying for this booking (a second tab, a double click)."""


class BookingService:
    def __init__(self, shows: dict[str, Show], seats: SeatLockService, gateway: PaymentGateway):
        self._shows = shows
        self._seats = seats
        self._gateway = gateway
        self._mutex = threading.Lock()                  # guards the three collections below
        self._bookings: dict[str, Booking] = {}
        self._results: dict[str, Booking] = {}          # idempotency key -> finished booking (prune after a day)
        self._paying: set[str] = set()                  # booking ids with a payment in flight

    def hold_seats(self, user_id: str, show_id: str, seat_ids: list[str]) -> Booking:
        show = self._shows[show_id]
        wanted = sorted(set(seat_ids))
        if not 0 < len(wanted) <= MAX_SEATS or any(s not in show.screen.seats for s in wanted):
            raise ValueError(f"pick 1 to {MAX_SEATS} seats that exist in {show.screen.name}")
        booking_id = uuid4().hex[:12]
        locks = self._seats.hold(show_id, wanted, user_id, booking_id)       # raises SeatUnavailable
        booking = Booking(booking_id, show_id, user_id, tuple(wanted),
                          amount=sum(show.price_of(s) for s in wanted), expires_at=locks[0].expires_at)
        with self._mutex:
            self._bookings[booking_id] = booking
        return booking

    def pay(self, booking_id: str, idempotency_key: str) -> Booking:
        with self._mutex:
            if idempotency_key in self._results:
                return self._results[idempotency_key]                    # a retry: replay, never charge again
            booking = self._bookings[booking_id]
            if booking.status is not BookingStatus.PENDING:
                return booking                                            # already decided
            if booking_id in self._paying:
                raise PaymentInProgress(booking_id)
            self._paying.add(booking_id)
        try:
            result = self._charge_and_commit(booking, idempotency_key)   # slow, so no lock is held
        except Exception:
            with self._mutex:
                self._paying.discard(booking_id)                          # nothing stored: a retry may try again
            raise
        with self._mutex:
            self._paying.discard(booking_id)
            self._results[idempotency_key] = result
        return result

    def _charge_and_commit(self, booking: Booking, key: str) -> Booking:
        show_id, seat_ids, booking_id = booking.show_id, booking.seat_ids, booking.booking_id
        if not self._seats.is_held(show_id, seat_ids, booking_id):
            self._transition(booking, BookingStatus.EXPIRED, BookingStatus.PENDING)
            return booking                                                # the hold ran out: take no money
        payment = self._gateway.charge(booking.amount, key)
        booking.payment = payment
        if payment.status is not PaymentStatus.CAPTURED:
            self._seats.release(show_id, seat_ids, booking_id)
            self._transition(booking, BookingStatus.FAILED, BookingStatus.PENDING, BookingStatus.EXPIRED)
        elif self._seats.commit(show_id, seat_ids, booking_id):          # still ours, or free again
            self._transition(booking, BookingStatus.CONFIRMED, BookingStatus.PENDING, BookingStatus.EXPIRED)
        else:                                                             # ran out mid-payment and was re-taken
            self._gateway.refund(payment.payment_id)
            payment.status = PaymentStatus.REFUNDED
            self._transition(booking, BookingStatus.EXPIRED, BookingStatus.PENDING)
        return booking

    def _transition(self, booking: Booking, to: BookingStatus, *allowed: BookingStatus) -> bool:
        with self._mutex:                               # the in-memory twin of UPDATE ... WHERE status IN (...)
            if booking.status not in allowed:
                return False
            booking.status = to
            return True

    def cancel(self, booking_id: str) -> Booking:
        with self._mutex:
            booking = self._bookings[booking_id]
        if self._transition(booking, BookingStatus.CANCELLED, BookingStatus.CONFIRMED):
            self._seats.unbook(booking.show_id, booking.seat_ids, booking.booking_id)
            if booking.payment is not None:
                self._gateway.refund(booking.payment.payment_id)         # cut-off and fee rules go here
                booking.payment.status = PaymentStatus.REFUNDED
        return booking

    def expire_stale(self) -> list[Booking]:
        """Run every minute or so. The seats are already free (lazy expiry); this marks bookings
        EXPIRED so that emails and the waitlist can react."""
        ended = {lock.booking_id for lock in self._seats.sweep_expired()}
        with self._mutex:
            stale = [self._bookings[b] for b in ended if b in self._bookings and b not in self._paying]
        return [b for b in stale if self._transition(b, BookingStatus.EXPIRED, BookingStatus.PENDING)]

import threading
from datetime import datetime, timedelta

from booking import BookingService
from models import BookingStatus, Movie, Payment, PaymentStatus, Screen, Seat, SeatType, Show
from seat_locks import SeatLockService, SeatUnavailable


class FakeClock:
    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self.now = datetime(2026, 1, 1, 18, 0)

    def __call__(self) -> datetime:
        return self.now

    def advance(self, minutes: float) -> None:
        self.now += timedelta(minutes=minutes)


class FakeGateway:
    """Charges once per idempotency key, as real gateways do. during_charge simulates a slow bank."""

    def __init__(self, decline: bool = False) -> None:
        self.decline = decline
        self.during_charge = None
        self.charges: dict[str, Payment] = {}
        self.refunds: list[str] = []
        self._lock = threading.Lock()

    def charge(self, amount: int, idempotency_key: str) -> Payment:
        if self.during_charge:
            self.during_charge()
        with self._lock:
            if idempotency_key not in self.charges:
                status = PaymentStatus.DECLINED if self.decline else PaymentStatus.CAPTURED
                self.charges[idempotency_key] = Payment(f"p{len(self.charges) + 1}", amount, idempotency_key, status)
            return self.charges[idempotency_key]

    def refund(self, payment_id: str) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            self.refunds.append(payment_id)


def make_system(decline: bool = False):
    clock = FakeClock()
    seats = {f"A{n}": Seat(f"A{n}", "A", n, SeatType.REGULAR) for n in range(1, 9)}
    show = Show("show-42", Movie("m1", "Interstellar", 169), Screen("s1", "Audi 1", seats),
                clock.now + timedelta(hours=2), {SeatType.REGULAR: 250})
    locks = SeatLockService(ttl=timedelta(minutes=10), clock=clock)
    gateway = FakeGateway(decline)
    return BookingService({show.show_id: show}, locks, gateway), locks, gateway, clock


def test_fifty_users_one_seat_exactly_one_hold():
    service, *_ = make_system()
    start, wins = threading.Barrier(50), []

    def attempt(n: int) -> None:
        start.wait()                                     # everybody clicks at the same moment
        try:
            service.hold_seats(f"user{n}", "show-42", ["A7"])
            wins.append(n)
        except SeatUnavailable:
            pass

    threads = [threading.Thread(target=attempt, args=(n,)) for n in range(50)]
    for th in threads:
        th.start()
    for th in threads:
        th.join()
    assert len(wins) == 1


def test_multi_seat_hold_is_all_or_nothing():
    service, locks, *_ = make_system()
    service.hold_seats("asha", "show-42", ["A2"])
    try:
        service.hold_seats("ravi", "show-42", ["A1", "A2", "A3"])
    except SeatUnavailable as exc:
        assert exc.seat_ids == ["A2"]                    # only the conflict is reported
    else:
        raise AssertionError("a partial hold must not succeed")
    assert locks.seat_map("show-42", ["A1", "A3"]) == {"A1": "available", "A3": "available"}

Why one mutex per show and not one per seat? A booking spans several seats, and all-or-nothing across per-seat locks needs a global lock order to avoid deadlock. A show has a few hundred seats and each critical section is a handful of dictionary operations, so one mutex per show costs almost nothing in contention and removes the ordering problem. Bookings for different shows never wait for each other.

A concurrency test is only worth having if it fails on broken code. Delete with state.mutex: in hold, add time.sleep(0.001) between the check and the write (real code does I/O there), and the first test fails: several of the 50 threads get A7.

Database-level guards

With several app servers the in-memory mutex coordinates nothing, because every process has its own. The database becomes the arbiter, and the classes map onto tables: SeatLockService becomes the show_seats table (one row per seat per show, created when the show is scheduled), and its methods become conditional statements like the ones above.

Extensions and interview practice

Extensions the interviewer may add (click each)

Dynamic pricing

Make price a Strategy, PricingPolicy.quote(show, seat, now) -> int, with implementations such as fixed per seat type, time of day, or occupancy-based (the price rises as the show fills). Two rules keep it honest. Quote at hold time and store the quote on the booking, so the price cannot change while the user is paying; then charge the stored quote, never a fresh one. The hold's TTL doubles as the quote's validity. Cap the multipliers, and compute occupancy from the seat state rather than from a counter that drifts.

Group bookings and best available

Holding N seats is already all-or-nothing. The new part is finding them: for best 4 together, scan each row's free seats for runs of length 4 or more (one linear pass per row) and score the candidates by distance from the centre of the screen. Many cinemas also refuse a selection that strands a single empty seat between taken ones, because nobody will buy it, so check the gap on each side of the run. Hold the chosen run atomically; if someone was faster, scan again rather than reporting an error.

Waitlist for sold-out shows

Users join a FIFO waitlist per show (optionally per seat type). Released seats are events (a hold that ran out, a declined payment, a cancellation), and the waitlist is an Observer of them. Offer released seats to the next person as a short priority hold, a SeatLock owned by the offer for, say, five minutes; if they do not pay, the seats pass to the next person. If waiters must come before browsers, the release has to turn the seat into an offer hold in the same atomic step; freeing it and then notifying lets anyone grab it first. This is also why a sweeper exists alongside lazy expiry: lazy expiry frees a seat silently, and a waitlist needs an event.

Cancellations, refunds and cut-offs

Cancelling is CONFIRMED → CANCELLED as a conditional update, allowed until a cut-off such as two hours before the show. In the same transaction mark the tickets cancelled (the partial unique index then lets the seat be sold again), set the show_seats rows back to AVAILABLE, and queue the refund through the outbox with its own idempotency key. Cancelling two of four seats needs a status per ticket rather than per booking: say so, and leave it out unless asked.

On-sale spikes: 100,000 people, one show

The seat rows of one show become the hottest rows in the database. Put a virtual waiting room in front that admits users at a rate the booking path can serve. Serve the seat map from a cache refreshed every second or two, since it is advisory anyway. Keep the hold a single conditional UPDATE so that row locks last microseconds. Rate-limit holds per user and cap seats per user per show so bots cannot hold a show hostage. If one database is not enough, shard by show: a booking never spans two shows, so the data splits cleanly.

'Walk me through your design.' A strong summary: 'Physical seats are catalogue data, and availability lives per show and seat. Selecting seats creates SeatLocks with a ten-minute TTL in one atomic, all-or-nothing step, so the second person is told immediately. Paying checks the hold, charges with an idempotency key while holding no lock, then commits with a conditional update that succeeds only if the seats are still ours or free. Every check ignores ended holds, and a sweeper sends the emails and feeds the waitlist. A late payment for a seat that someone else now holds is refunded automatically. In the database every transition is a conditional UPDATE, with a unique index on live tickets per show and seat as the backstop.'

Answers that lose points

  • An is_booked flag on Seat, which every show in the screen shares
  • Python locks only, with several app servers behind a load balancer
  • A transaction or row lock held while the user pays
  • Holds with no expiry, or an expiry that only a cron job enforces
  • Charging before checking the hold, or sending tickets from both the webhook and the redirect
  • A Redis lock as the only guard, with no database check at commit

from __future__ import annotations

import threading
from collections.abc import Callable
from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from datetime import datetime, timedelta
from enum import Enum
from uuid import uuid4


class SeatUnavailable(Exception):
    pass


class Status(Enum):
    PENDING = "pending"
    CONFIRMED = "confirmed"
    EXPIRED = "expired"
    FAILED = "failed"


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Hold:
    booking_id: str
    expires_at: datetime


@dataclass
class Booking:
    booking_id: str
    show_id: str
    seats: tuple[str, ...]
    amount: int
    status: Status = Status.PENDING


@dataclass
class ShowSeats:                                       # one show's seat state, guarded by its own lock
    lock: threading.Lock = field(default_factory=threading.Lock)
    holds: dict[str, Hold] = field(default_factory=dict)
    booked: dict[str, str] = field(default_factory=dict)


class Cinema:
    """prices: show_id -> {seat_id: price}. gateway.charge(amount, key) -> bool; gateway.refund(key)."""

    def __init__(self, prices: dict[str, dict[str, int]], gateway, ttl: timedelta = timedelta(minutes=10),
                 clock: Callable[[], datetime] = datetime.now):
        self._prices, self._gateway, self._ttl, self._clock = prices, gateway, ttl, clock
        self._shows = {show_id: ShowSeats() for show_id in prices}     # created up front: no registry lock
        self._mutex = threading.Lock()                                   # guards the three collections below
        self._bookings: dict[str, Booking] = {}
        self._by_key: dict[str, Status] = {}
        self._paying: set[str] = set()

    @staticmethod
    def _free(st: ShowSeats, seat: str, booking_id: str, now: datetime) -> bool:
        if seat in st.booked:
            return st.booked[seat] == booking_id
        hold = st.holds.get(seat)
        return hold is None or hold.booking_id == booking_id or hold.expires_at <= now

    def hold(self, show_id: str, seat_ids: list[str]) -> str:
        prices = self._prices[show_id]
        wanted = tuple(sorted(set(seat_ids)))
        if not wanted or any(s not in prices for s in wanted):
            raise ValueError("unknown seats")
        booking_id, st = uuid4().hex, self._shows[show_id]
        with st.lock:                                                    # check and write in one critical section
            now = self._clock()
            if not all(self._free(st, s, booking_id, now) for s in wanted):
                raise SeatUnavailable(wanted)
            for s in wanted:
                st.holds[s] = Hold(booking_id, now + self._ttl)
        with self._mutex:
            self._bookings[booking_id] = Booking(booking_id, show_id, wanted, sum(prices[s] for s in wanted))
        return booking_id

    def seat_map(self, show_id: str) -> dict[str, str]:
        st = self._shows[show_id]
        with st.lock:
            now = self._clock()
            return {s: "booked" if s in st.booked else
                       "held" if s in st.holds and st.holds[s].expires_at > now else "available"
                    for s in self._prices[show_id]}

    def pay(self, booking_id: str, key: str) -> Status:
        with self._mutex:
            if key in self._by_key:
                return self._by_key[key]                                 # a retry: replay the first answer
            b = self._bookings[booking_id]
            if b.status is not Status.PENDING or booking_id in self._paying:
                return b.status
            self._paying.add(booking_id)
        try:
            status = self._settle(b, key)
        finally:
            with self._mutex:
                self._paying.discard(booking_id)
        with self._mutex:
            b.status = status
            self._by_key[key] = status
        return status

    def _settle(self, b: Booking, key: str) -> Status:
        st = self._shows[b.show_id]
        with st.lock:
            now = self._clock()
            live = all(s in st.holds and st.holds[s].booking_id == b.booking_id and st.holds[s].expires_at > now
                       for s in b.seats)
        if not live:
            return Status.EXPIRED                                        # never charge for a hold that has ended
        if not self._gateway.charge(b.amount, key):                      # slow call: no lock held
            with st.lock:
                for s in b.seats:
                    if s in st.holds and st.holds[s].booking_id == b.booking_id:
                        del st.holds[s]
            return Status.FAILED
        with st.lock:
            now = self._clock()
            if all(self._free(st, s, b.booking_id, now) for s in b.seats):
                for s in b.seats:
                    st.holds.pop(s, None)
                    st.booked[s] = b.booking_id
                return Status.CONFIRMED
        self._gateway.refund(key)                                        # ended and re-taken: give the money back
        return Status.EXPIRED


if __name__ == "__main__":
    class Gateway:
        def __init__(self):
            self.charged, self.refunded = set(), []

        def charge(self, amount, key):
            self.charged.add(key)                                        # a set: one charge per key
            return True

        def refund(self, key):
            self.refunded.append(key)

    now = [datetime(2026, 1, 1, 18, 0)]
    gateway = Gateway()
    cinema = Cinema({"s42": {f"A{i}": 250 for i in range(1, 9)}}, gateway, clock=lambda: now[0])
    wins, gate = [], threading.Barrier(50)

    def attempt():
        gate.wait()
        try:
            wins.append(cinema.hold("s42", ["A7"]))
        except SeatUnavailable:
            pass

    threads = [threading.Thread(target=attempt) for _ in range(50)]
    for th in threads:
        th.start()
    for th in threads:
        th.join()
    assert len(wins) == 1
    assert cinema.pay(wins[0], "k1") is Status.CONFIRMED
    assert cinema.pay(wins[0], "k1") is Status.CONFIRMED and gateway.charged == {"k1"}   # replayed, not re-charged
    b2 = cinema.hold("s42", ["A1", "A2"])
    now[0] += timedelta(minutes=11)                                      # walked away
    assert cinema.seat_map("s42")["A1"] == "available"                   # lazy expiry: no sweeper needed
    assert cinema.pay(b2, "k2") is Status.EXPIRED and "k2" not in gateway.charged
    print("all checks passed")
